Computational Geodynamics · McNair Research

Tungsten-182 Isotopic Anomalies and Mantle Dynamics

Modeling early Earth mantle evolution using numerical simulations.

Project Overview

This project investigated the origin of tungsten-182 isotopic anomalies observed in mantle-derived rocks. Under the mentorship of Prof. Katherine Bermingham and Prof. Charles-Édouard Boukaré, I used geodynamic simulations to test how late accretion, core–mantle interaction, silicate differentiation, and mantle mixing could affect the preservation and evolution of tungsten isotopic signatures over Earth history.

Methods

  • Modified Fortran source code to implement alternative mantle evolution scenarios.
  • Ran large-scale simulations on Graham and Cedar high-performance computing clusters.
  • Analyzed simulation outputs using Python statistical routines.
  • Compared modeled tungsten-182 signatures with observational constraints from mantle-derived rocks.
  • Presented results through oral, poster, and group-meeting formats.

Research Significance

Tungsten-182 anomalies preserve information about early Earth differentiation, accretion, and long-term mantle evolution. This project gave me direct experience testing physical hypotheses through numerical simulations, modifying scientific code, managing HPC workflows, and interpreting model outputs against geochemical observations.
