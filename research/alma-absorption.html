ALMA CO Absorption in Active Galaxies

Searching for faint molecular absorption lines against bright AGN continua.

Status Expanding sample size.
Advisor Dr. Benjamin Boizelle
Data Archival ALMA observations
Core output CO/H2 molecular gas constraints

Project overview

Cold gas seen in absorption against active nuclei.

This project uses archival ALMA observations to search for carbon monoxide absorption lines against compact radio cores in active galaxies. With emphasis on weak and narrow absorption features, including components with velocity dispersions σv < 10 km s-1, that may be missed in higher-binned observations. Regardless, these features trace cold molecular gas along the line of sight and can reveal the kinematics, column densities, excitation conditions, and structure of gas in AGN environments.

I began this work as an NSF REU research intern at Brigham Young University under the mentorship of Dr. Benjamin Boizelle during the summer of 2025. After completion of the program, we continued working together throughout my senior year, which resulted in an honors thesis report. Currently, we are increasing our sample size further to uncover more of the fainter CO absorption features.

My role

Independent analysis with weekly faculty mentorship.

I am carrying out the core analysis for this project through a fitting model code that I built and developed from scratch. The model itself includes a rigorous Gaussian line fitting model, Monte Carlo error procedure, which will be expanded further to include Hann smoothing, measurable cloud properties, and detailed plotting functions. Between weekly advisor meetings, I developed the analysis, prepared figures, identified data issues, asked targeted questions, and converted progress into poster and thesis material.

Methods

Observational ALMA data to gas properties.

  • Uncovered promising galaxy targets with high enough resolution for fainter features.
  • Downloaded the raw observational data and extracted measurement sets with appropriate CASA pipeline versions.
  • Applied phase and amplitude self-calibration and imaged/cleaned spectral cubes.
  • Extracted spectra against compact AGN continuum sources.
  • Normalized spectra using polynomial continuum-fitting procedures.
  • Modeled faint absorption features with a Gaussian fitting model.
  • Estimated integrated opacities, CO column densities, and H2 column densities.
  • Compared line properties across multiple CO transitions where available.

Why it matters

Radio absorption lines are direct probes of intervening molecular gas.

Molecular absorption lines provide a direct method for probing cold gas in front of bright continuum sources. In nearby AGN, this allows the properties and dynamics of molecular gas to be studied in detail. Long term, this work connects to my broader interest in using absorption-line spectroscopy to study the gas environments and evolution of high-redshift quasars.
