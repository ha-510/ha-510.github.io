Current Research · Senior Honors Thesis

ALMA CO Absorption in Active Galaxies

Searching for faint molecular absorption lines against bright AGN continua.

Project Overview

This project uses archival ALMA observations to search for carbon monoxide absorption lines against compact radio cores in active galaxies. These absorption features trace cold molecular gas along the line of sight and can reveal the kinematics, column densities, excitation conditions, and structure of gas near active galactic nuclei.

I began this work as an NSF REU research intern at Brigham Young University under the mentorship of Dr. Benjamin Boizelle. The project is continuing as the basis of my senior honors thesis at Rutgers University.

Methods

  • Processed and analyzed archival ALMA observations of active galaxies.
  • Extracted spectra against bright AGN continuum sources.
  • Normalized spectra using continuum-fitting procedures.
  • Modeled absorption features with Gaussian fitting routines.
  • Estimated integrated opacities, CO column densities, and H2 column densities.
  • Compared line properties across multiple CO transitions where available.

Research Significance

Molecular absorption lines provide a direct method for probing cold gas in front of bright continuum sources. In nearby AGN, this allows the structure and motion of circumnuclear gas to be studied in detail. Long term, this work connects to my broader interest in using absorption-line spectroscopy to study the gas environments of high-redshift quasars and the evolution of massive black holes.
