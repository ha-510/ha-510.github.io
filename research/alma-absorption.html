ALMA CO Absorption in Active Galaxies

Searching for faint molecular absorption lines against bright AGN continua.

Status Ongoing honors thesis
Advisor Dr. Benjamin Boizelle
Data Archival ALMA observations
Core output CO and H2 gas constraints

Project overview

Cold gas seen in absorption against active nuclei.

This project uses archival ALMA observations to search for carbon monoxide absorption lines against compact radio cores in active galaxies. These absorption features trace cold molecular gas along the line of sight and can reveal the kinematics, column densities, excitation conditions, and structure of gas near active galactic nuclei.

I began this work as an NSF REU research intern at Brigham Young University under the mentorship of Dr. Benjamin Boizelle. The project is continuing as the basis of my senior honors thesis at Rutgers University.

My role

Independent analysis with weekly faculty mentorship.

I carried out the core analysis for this project, including archival data inspection, CASA imaging, spectral extraction, continuum normalization, Gaussian absorption-line fitting, uncertainty estimation, and physical interpretation of the resulting line properties. Between weekly advisor meetings, I developed the analysis, prepared figures, identified data issues, asked targeted questions, and converted progress into poster and thesis material.

Methods

From ALMA data to gas properties.

  • Processed and inspected archival ALMA observations of nearby active galaxies.
  • Performed CASA imaging and worked with spectral cubes and continuum images.
  • Extracted spectra against compact AGN continuum sources.
  • Normalized spectra using continuum-fitting procedures.
  • Modeled faint absorption features with Gaussian fitting routines.
  • Estimated integrated opacities, CO column densities, and H2 column densities.
  • Compared line properties across multiple CO transitions where available.

Technical contributions

What this project trained.

  • ALMA imaging and spectral-cube handling.
  • Target-by-target spectral analysis workflows.
  • Monte Carlo uncertainty estimation for fitted line properties.
  • Detection and non-detection assessment across heterogeneous datasets.
  • Scientific figures, AAS-style tables, posters, and thesis writing.

Why it matters

Absorption lines are direct probes of intervening gas.

Molecular absorption lines provide a direct method for probing cold gas in front of bright continuum sources. In nearby AGN, this allows the structure and motion of circumnuclear gas to be studied in detail. Long term, this work connects to my broader interest in using absorption-line spectroscopy to study the gas environments of high-redshift quasars and the evolution of massive black holes.
