Hammad Abdullah

Undergraduate astrophysics researcher studying cold gas, active galaxies, and the environments of supermassive black holes.

I am a B.S. student in Astrophysics at Rutgers University–New Brunswick, with a minor in Mathematics, and a Ronald E. McNair Scholar. My research combines observational astronomy, spectral-line analysis, and computational modeling. I am currently conducting an ALMA carbon monoxide absorption-line survey of active galaxies as the basis of my senior honors thesis, while building toward graduate research on high-redshift quasars, AGN fueling, feedback, and the gas environments surrounding massive black holes.

Research Mission

My long-term goal is to understand how supermassive black holes formed, grew, and shaped their environments across cosmic time. I am especially interested in using absorption-line spectroscopy and numerical modeling to connect the small-scale physics of gas near active galactic nuclei to the large-scale evolution of galaxies and the intergalactic medium.
