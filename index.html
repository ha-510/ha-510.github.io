Current Research

Faint CO absorption in nearby active galaxies.

I am conducting a census of faint CO absorption features in archival ALMA observations of nearby (z < 0.03), active, early-type galaxies. This work involves ALMA imaging and cleaning of archival, raw observational data, spectral analysis of continuum-normalized fluxes, Gaussian line fitting on emission/absorption features, Monte Carlo error propagation for best-fit parameters, and estimates of detected absorbing gas properties.

This project connects my current observational work to broader questions about AGN fueling, feedback, molecular gas reservoirs, and the environments in which supermassive black holes grow.

Origin

From a dome to radio astronomy.

During pre-K, in Minue School of Carteret, NJ, I crawled into an inflatable planetarium dome and bore witness to an artificial night sky. The sheer scale, beauty, and perfection that lay before me was enough to fuel a lifetime supply of curiosity and infatuation with the heavens above. Beginning with the fundamental thought of what exactly is out there, across the vast cosmic abyss, to now thinking deeply about how spectra, in the radio regime, reveal the underlying properties of cold molecular gas and their role in the accretion of active black holes.

Research Direction

From molecular absorption lines to high-redshift quasar environments.

My long-term goal is to understand how supermassive black holes accumulated gas, grew rapidly, and became luminous quasars in the early Universe. I am especially interested in using spectral-line observations with numerical modeling to connect observed cold gas properties and distributions to theoretical models of AGN accretion, feedback, and growth across cosmic time.

Research Approach

Independent, quantitative, and communication-driven.

In my major research projects, I have worked independently between weekly faculty meetings: developing code and analysis routines, preparing figures, presenting progress, and turning results into posters, reports, talks, and thesis materials.

I am especially comfortable moving between data reduction, visualization, statistical uncertainty estimation, physical interpretation, and written/presented scientific communication.
