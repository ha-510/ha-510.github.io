My current research is a faint CO absorption-line census of nearby (z < 0.03), active, early galaxies using archival ALMA observations. I am building from this foundation toward graduate research on high-redshift quasars, AGN fueling and feedback, and the overall gas environments of supermassive black holes.

Research mission

From absorption features to supermassive black-hole environments.

My long-term goal is to understand how supermassive black holes formed, grew, and ignited as quasars in the early Universe.

Research Approach

Indepednet deep thinker

In both major research projects, I worked independently between weekly faculty meetings: developing analyses, asking targeted questions, presenting progress, and turning results into posters, reports, talks, and thesis material.

Research arc

A coherent path, not disconnected projects.

01

Observe

Use ALMA spectra to identify faint molecular absorption against compact AGN continuum sources.

02

Measure

Fit line profiles, estimate uncertainties, derive opacities and column densities, and separate detections from artifacts.

03

Model

Build synthetic line-of-sight spectra to test how gas geometry, optical depth, velocity structure, and resolution shape observations.

04

Extend

Apply the same observational and modeling logic to high-redshift quasars, AGN feedback, and early black-hole growth.

Origin

The question has become sharper over time.

My interest in astronomy began with a childhood planetarium dome in Carteret, New Jersey. The early question was broad: what is out there? The current version is narrower and research-grade: what can spectra reveal about the gas that feeds, obscures, and is shaped by active black holes? That is the line connecting my childhood curiosity, my ALMA work, my numerical modeling background, and the graduate research I intend to pursue.
